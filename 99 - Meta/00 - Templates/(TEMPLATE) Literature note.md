# Literature note structure

## Source and reading coverage

Record the title, authors, year, citation key and DOI or source link. Identify the version consulted.

State which sections have actually been read and which remain unread.

## Why I am reading this

Identify the question this source may help answer.

## What the source establishes

Summarise its central argument, method and results. Distinguish the authors’ conclusions from personal interpretation.

## Assumptions and conventions

Record the definitions needed to interpret or reproduce the relevant results: notation, units, approximations, parameter choices and the scope of the work.

## Detailed annotations

Organise notes by topic or source section.

For each important passage, equation or figure, record its location, explain what it means and add any questions or checks.

## How this relates to my work

### The question it helps answer

Identify the specific question or methodological choice.

### What I can use

Name the result, derivation, definition, method or comparison that is useful, with its source location.

### Where it enters my work

Identify the relevant topic note, draft section or project step.

### What differs from my work

Record differences in assumptions, definitions, conventions, scope or method.

### What needs checking before I use it

Identify conversions, derivations, reproductions or additional evidence required.

### What this source does not establish

State the limits of its support for the claim being considered.

## Connections to other sources

Explain whether another source supplies background, extends the result, uses a different convention or reaches a conflicting conclusion.

## Open questions and next reading

Record unresolved points and the sources needed to investigate them.
