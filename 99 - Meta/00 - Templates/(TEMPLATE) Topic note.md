# Topic notebook structure

## What am I trying to understand?

State the questions driving this note and why they matter for the work. These can be broader than the eventual write-up.

## Background and intuition

Explain the idea in ordinary language. Include prerequisites, useful examples and the picture behind the details.

## Working through the details

Keep intermediate steps, definitions, conventions and assumptions. Explain steps that would be compressed or omitted in the final write-up.

## Reading and annotations

For each relevant source, link to its literature note and record the specific point being used.

Keep direct quotations separate from paraphrases and personal comments. Include page, section, equation or figure identifiers.

## How the ideas connect

Include a small concept map and explain the relationships in words. Label connections with phrases such as “depends on”, “assumes”, “explains” or “is tested by”.

Link to existing concept notes when they provide useful background.

## My current understanding

Record the interpretation reached so far, what changed it and what remains uncertain.

Dated entries can preserve changes in understanding without erasing earlier reasoning.

## How this affects my work

Explain what the topic changes about the project.

Separate an adopted choice from a possibility that still needs investigation.

## Questions and checks

Keep unresolved derivations, conflicting conventions, missing evidence and questions for discussion here.

## What may go into the write-up

Collect the argument, evidence and references that are ready for formal writing. Retain the surrounding exploratory material elsewhere in this note.
